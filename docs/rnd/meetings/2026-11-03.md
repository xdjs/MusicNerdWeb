## Details

### Gatherings as a Music Nerd experience

Swang provided a reference for accessible, social gatherings where people can arrive alone and choose how much to participate. Applied to music, the opportunity is a hosted gathering with shared activities and room to move between listening, creating and conversation. Creative camps illustrate how intentionally bringing people together can produce both music and lasting collaborations.

Recurring gatherings, discussed through the “Topia” idea and Latasha’s work, could give fans a clearer sense of format and duration. Playlists, recordings and other artifacts become more meaningful when connected to the people and event that produced them. These were experience ideas, not an approved event plan or a commitment to on-chain infrastructure.

### Capture the community’s knowledge

Underground scenes often lack someone documenting the creative process as it happens. The team discussed tools that let artists or archivists capture photos, recordings, context and relationships in the room, then contribute that material to Music Nerd without relying only on manual link entry.

Start with artists and histories the team knows well. Capture creative chapters, collaborations and less visible contributors, including executive producers and local industry connections. An artist’s history and the creative world they are building are related but distinct stories. Human stewardship remains necessary to verify identity, preserve context and resolve conflicting information.

Music Nerd could eventually support tailored stories or longer publications from its knowledge base. That is an aspiration, not a feature delivered by this meeting.

### Demonstrate one small slice in depth

A fully developed scene, era or connected group of artists would make Music Nerd’s value easier to understand than a broad set of features alone. Examples discussed included the music NFT scene, the Beatles and Baroque music; the final subject was not selected.

The preferred direction was to explore experiences on top of the database rather than place every experience inside the current Music Nerd website. The website can support creating, inspecting and maintaining knowledge while other interfaces make that knowledge engaging. Existing archives should be researched for useful presentation patterns. A prototype by the end of the week was floated, but no firm deadline or owner was confirmed.

### Onboarding needs reliable identity and review

Pete described an underground-artist onboarding attempt that returned incorrect links, including the wrong YouTube account. The rough accuracy estimate in the conversation was anecdotal, not a measured evaluation. Name ambiguity and a limited public footprint make confident automatic matching unreliable.

The desired behavior is to acknowledge missing knowledge rather than invent matches. Candidate confirmation, such as asking whether a result is the correct artist, was proposed; the meeting did not establish that prompting alone would solve the problem.

Three scenarios were agreed as the next onboarding feature direction:

1. New artist claims an unknown profile: research runs, the profile fills in, and an artist-facing interview follows.
2. A whitelisted contributor adds an unknown artist: use the same visible research and profile-building flow, followed by questions about what the contributor knows about that artist.
3. An artist claims an existing populated profile: review what Music Nerd already knows, correct or add information, and continue through an interview instead of repeating the initial research simply because the claim is new.

Non-whitelisted users should also be able to add artists. A guided verification mechanism is needed so contributors check links and complete relevant follow-up steps instead of assuming every generated result is correct. A checklist was suggested; its exact design remains open.

Pete identified [#1424](https://github.com/xdjs/MusicNerdWeb/issues/1424) as his pressing priority: get PDFs working and retrieve information already present in Lore without unnecessary web searches or delays. These are meeting-reported priorities, not confirmation that the work has shipped.

Recovering missing catalog counterparts and revalidating historical Apple Music IDs was deprioritized in the discussion. Removing that work from the board was proposed, not verified as completed.

Pete’s cloud-agent setup was still waiting on environment configuration. The discussion favored reusing existing development configuration. Completing setup remains a follow-up; no credentials or environment values are recorded here.

The FEED//FWD episode still needed a full review of its animated moments before Pete could approve it.

## Agreed direction

- Begin with the immediate community and a small, deeply documented example.
- Prioritize the three onboarding scenarios, sharing the research flow while adapting the interview to the person contributing.
- Let a claiming artist review existing knowledge rather than automatically repeat completed onboarding research.
- Support non-whitelisted artist additions with a verification mechanism; checklist details remain to be designed.
- Keep ad hoc work visible through an owning issue.
